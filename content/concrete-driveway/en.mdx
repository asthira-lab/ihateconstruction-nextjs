## What is a concrete driveway?

A concrete driveway is a flat slab of concrete poured over a prepared subgrade that cars and trucks drive on. Pouring one means ordering concrete by **volume** — the footprint (length × width) times its thickness. Get that wrong and you either run short mid-pour or overpay for a truck you didn't need.

This calculator turns length, width, and thickness into what you actually order: **cubic metres or cubic yards** of ready-mix, the **number of premix bags** (80 lb or 60 lb), the **wire mesh or rebar** for crack control, the **control joint spacing** to guide where the slab cracks, and the **ready-mix truck loads** you'll need.

## How to use the concrete driveway calculator

1. Enter the **length** and **width** of the driveway footprint. Any unit works — metres, feet, centimetres, inches, or millimetres.
2. Enter the **thickness** of the slab. Typical residential driveways are 100–150 mm (4–6 in); use 150 mm (6 in) for regular car use and up to 200 mm (8 in) for heavy vehicles.
3. Choose a **reinforcement** option — none, wire mesh, or rebar — and set the **rebar size and spacing** if you pick rebar. Reinforcement controls cracking, it does not add significant strength to a driveway.
4. Set the **control joint spacing** so the saw-cut joints line up with the slab thickness in feet — a common rule of thumb.
5. Adjust the **wastage** if you expect a bumpy subgrade, spillage, or formwork over-dig. 5% is a sensible default for a flat site.
6. Press **Calculate** and read off the volume, bag count, reinforcement, and truck count.

## Worked example

A 20 ft × 10 ft driveway, 4 in thick, no reinforcement:

- Area = 200 ft²
- Thickness = 4 in = 0.333 ft
- Volume = 200 × 0.333 = 66.7 ft³ ≈ **2.47 yd³** (1.89 m³)
- With 5% wastage ≈ **2.59 yd³** (1.98 m³)
- 80 lb premix bags: 2.59 yd³ ÷ 0.0222 yd³ per bag ≈ **117 bags**
- Ready-mix: 2.59 yd³ ÷ 7 yd³ per truck ≈ **0.4 of a truck**

For a bigger pour, a 6 m × 4 m driveway at 150 mm thick works out to 3.6 m³ — about 4.7 yd³, which is roughly two-thirds of a single 7 yd³ ready-mix load.

## Notes and caveats

- The volume is calculated from **nominal dimensions** — it assumes a perfectly flat slab of uniform thickness. Real subgrades settle, formwork bulges, and screeding eats into the top, so always order 5–10% extra.
- Bag counts assume a standard premix yield of **0.6 ft³ per 80 lb bag** and **0.45 ft³ per 60 lb bag**. Actual yield varies by brand and how wet you mix it.
- Weight uses a **2400 kg/m³** density, typical for standard reinforced concrete.
- Control joints should be spaced roughly equal to the slab thickness in feet (about 5 m apart for a 150 mm slab) and placed at chokepoints like corners of the garage door.
- This calculator sizes the concrete for the driveway body only. It does **not** include footings, haunches, thickening under load-bearing walls, or concrete for a curb — add those volumes separately.
- For structural driveways carrying unusual loads, confirm mix grade and reinforcement with a structural engineer. The numbers here are for ordering materials, not for structural design.
