## What this brick calculator handles

- **Brick wall** — straight, corner, or partition wall. Enter length, height, and thickness; doors and windows subtract from the area.
- **Brick patio / paving** — switch to volume mode with a shallow thickness (2–3 inches for pavers) to get the paver count.
- **Fire pit** — circular rings: compute the circumference, multiply by height and thickness, and enter as volume. Use the fire brick preset.
- **Retaining wall** — enter the real wall thickness; retaining walls are usually double a partition wall.
- **Whole house** — total your external wall length, subtract openings, and enter as one wall.
- **By square feet** — enter area as length × 1 ft with height = area ÷ length.

## How the brick calculation works

1. **Masonry volume** = wall length × height × thickness, with openings (doors, windows) subtracted from the wall area first.
2. **Nominal brick volume** = (brick length + joint) × (brick width + joint) × (brick height + joint) — what one brick actually consumes on the wall, mortar included.
3. **Bricks required** = masonry volume ÷ nominal brick volume, rounded up, then wastage added on top.
4. **Mortar volume** = masonry volume − (bricks × actual brick volume), multiplied by (1 + mortar wastage %) then by 1.33 to convert wet to dry.
5. **Cement and sand** split from the dry mortar by the ratio (default 1:6). Cement converts to 50 kg bags; sand converts to cubic feet.

## Brick presets

- **Modular brick (190×90×90 mm)** — IS 1077 with a 10 mm joint, the standard most engineers use.
- **Traditional Indian brick (230×110×75 mm)** — the common non-modular size on site.
- **AAC block (600×200×200 mm)** — a much larger, lighter unit with a thin 3 mm joint.

## Worked example

A **10 m × 3 m** wall, **230 mm** thick, with one **0.9 m × 2.1 m** door.

- Wall area = 10 × 3 − (0.9 × 2.1) = **28.11 m²**
- Masonry volume = 28.11 × 0.23 = **6.47 m³**
- Modular brick nominal volume ≈ 0.2 × 0.1 × 0.1 = **0.002 m³**
- Bricks = 6.47 ÷ 0.002 ≈ **3,235 bricks** before wastage
