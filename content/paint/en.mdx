## How the paint quantity is calculated

1. **Wall area** = 2 × (length + width) × height. Tick "include ceiling" to add length × width on top.
2. **Openings** (doors and windows) are subtracted as rectangles to give the net paintable area.
3. For each layer, **area covered** = net area × number of coats.
4. **Litres before wastage** = area covered ÷ coverage per litre (12 sqm/L for interior emulsion by default).
5. **Litres** = litres before wastage × (1 + wastage %). Default wastage is 5–7% depending on the paint type.
6. Totals across layers give the full paint order for the job.

## Paint types

- **Interior emulsion** — ~12 sqm/L coverage, two coats standard.
- **Exterior emulsion** — lower coverage, weather-resistant.
- **Primer** — seals the surface before the finish coat.
- **Wall putty** — quoted in kg/sqm (about 20–25 sqm per kg).
- **Sealer** — seals porous or chalky surfaces.

## Coats guidance

- Two finish coats is the industry default — one coat rarely gives even colour.
- Bump to three for deep colours (dark red, navy) or high-traffic areas.
- Add one primer coat on a fresh or puttied wall; optional for repaints over sound emulsion.

## Worked example

A room **4.5 m × 3.6 m × 3.0 m** with one **0.9 m × 2.1 m** door.

- Wall area = 2 × (4.5 + 3.6) × 3.0 = **48.6 m²**
- Net area = 48.6 − (0.9 × 2.1) = **46.71 m²**
- Two coats of interior emulsion: 46.71 × 2 = **93.42 m²**
- Litres = 93.42 ÷ 12 ≈ **7.8 L** before wastage
