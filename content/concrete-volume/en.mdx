## What is concrete volume?

Concrete volume is the amount of poured concrete a structure needs, measured in cubic metres (m³), cubic feet (ft³), or cubic yards (yd³). Because concrete is ordered and priced by volume — not by weight or area — getting this number right is the difference between a full pour and a short pour.

Volume is simple geometry: a slab or footing is a box (**length × width × depth**), and a round post hole is a cylinder (**π × (diameter/2)² × depth**). The only real skill is keeping every dimension in the same unit before multiplying.

## How to use the concrete volume calculator

Three steps are enough to plan a concrete order:

1. **Pick the shape** — rectangular for a slab, footing, or wall; cylindrical for a round post hole.
2. **Enter the dimensions** — length and width plus the thickness for a slab, or diameter and depth for a post hole. Any unit works (mm, cm, m, ft, in).
3. **Set the wastage** — add 5–10% for uneven ground, over-dig, spillage, and formwork waste, then press Calculate.

The calculator returns the volume before wastage and with wastage in all three units (cubic metres, cubic feet, and cubic yards) plus the estimated weight in kilograms and tonnes.

## Worked example

Slab **6.0 m × 4.0 m**, **150 mm thick**, **5% wastage**.

Convert the thickness to metres first: 150 mm = 0.15 m. Then:

- Volume = 6.0 × 4.0 × 0.15 = **3.60 m³**
- In cubic feet = 3.60 × 35.31 = **127.1 ft³**
- In cubic yards = 3.60 × 1.308 = **4.71 yd³**
- With 5% wastage = 3.60 × 1.05 = **3.78 m³**
- Weight at 2400 kg/m³ = 3.78 × 2400 = **9,072 kg** (about 9.1 tonnes)

A ready-mix supplier would quote that as roughly **5 yd³** — the closest whole-yard amount above 4.71.

## Concrete volume conversion reference

Handy numbers when you need to convert without the calculator:

- **1 cubic yard = 27 cubic feet** = 0.7646 cubic metres
- **1 cubic metre = 1.308 cubic yards** = 35.31 cubic feet
- **1 cubic foot = 0.0283 cubic metres**
- A typical ready-mix truck carries **9–12 yd³**

## Notes and caveats

- Volume assumes nominal, straight-sided dimensions. Irregular or stepped footings and sloped slabs need more careful measurement.
- Add wastage on top of the theoretical volume — **5%** is normal for a level slab on prepared ground; **10%** is safer for post holes, steep sites, and hand mixing.
- Weight uses standard reinforced-concrete density of **2400 kg/m³**. Lightweight mixes and heavy-aggregate mixes differ.
- For slabs thicker than ~200 mm or in seismic zones, reinforcement, compaction, and mix design are engineering decisions — volume alone is not a full concrete spec.
