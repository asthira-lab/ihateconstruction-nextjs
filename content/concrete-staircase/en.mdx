## What is a Concrete Staircase Calculator?

A concrete staircase calculator helps you estimate the concrete volume and material quantities (cement, sand, aggregate) needed to build a straight-run concrete staircase. It uses the rise (step height), run (step depth), staircase width, and number of steps to compute the total volume, then applies a standard 1:2:4 mix ratio to give you the exact number of cement bags and volumes of sand and aggregate required.

## How to use the Concrete Staircase Calculator

1. Enter the **rise** — the vertical height of each step (typically 150–200 mm for residential).
2. Enter the **run** — the horizontal depth of each step (typically 250–300 mm).
3. Enter the **width** — the horizontal width of the staircase flight.
4. Enter the **step count** — total number of steps in the flight.
5. Adjust the **wastage** percentage if needed (default 5% accounts for spillage and formwork gaps).
6. Click **Calculate** to see concrete volume and material breakdown.

## Worked Example

A straight-run staircase with:
- Rise: 175 mm
- Run: 280 mm
- Width: 1,000 mm (1 m)
- Steps: 12

**Step volume** = 0.175 × 0.280 × 1.000 = 0.049 m³ per step  
**Total volume** = 0.049 × 12 = 0.588 m³  
**With 5% wastage** = 0.617 m³

**1:2:4 Mix Breakdown** (cement:sand:aggregate):
- Cement: 0.088 m³ → 127 kg → 6 bags (25 kg) or 3 bags (50 kg)
- Sand: 0.176 m³
- Aggregate: 0.353 m³

## Notes and Caveats

- **Rectangular prism assumption** — Each step is treated as a solid rectangular block. Real stairs often have stringers, landings, or tapered treads that change the volume.
- **Mix ratio** — Uses standard 1:2:4 (cement:sand:aggregate) by volume. Adjust for your local standard or design mix.
- **Densities** — Cement 1,440 kg/m³, sand 1,600 kg/m³, aggregate 1,600 kg/m³. Actual densities vary by source.
- **Wastage** — Default 5% covers normal spillage. Increase for complex formwork or pump placement.
- **Structural design** — This calculator estimates *materials only*. It does not check reinforcement, load capacity, or code compliance. Always consult a structural engineer for load-bearing stairs.
- **Landings & stringers** — Add separate volumes for landings, stringer beams, or waist slabs if your design includes them.
